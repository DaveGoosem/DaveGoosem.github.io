DaveGoosem.com is a personal blog run by David Goosem. This page explains what information is collected when you visit, why, and what you can do about it. The short version: the site has no accounts, forms, newsletters or advertising, and it never sells your data.

## What is collected

**Analytics (Google Analytics 4).** The site uses Google Analytics to understand which posts people read and how they find them. Google Analytics sets cookies and collects information such as the pages you view, how long you stay, the site that referred you, your browser and device type, and your approximate location (city or country level). It is used only for aggregate reporting on what content is useful. See [how Google uses information from sites that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

**Performance measurement (Vercel Speed Insights).** The site measures page performance (Core Web Vitals such as load time) so it stays fast. Vercel Speed Insights doesn't use cookies, and the data is aggregated rather than tied to individual visitors.

**Hosting.** The site is hosted on [Vercel](https://vercel.com). As with any website, Vercel's servers process technical request data such as your IP address and browser user agent to deliver pages and protect against abuse. See [Vercel's privacy notice](https://vercel.com/legal/privacy-policy).

## What is not collected

- There are no sign-ups, comment forms or newsletters, so the site doesn't ask for your name or email address.
- Site search runs entirely in your browser; your search terms aren't sent anywhere.
- Fonts are served from this site, not loaded from a third-party font service.
- There's no advertising and no ad-tracking.

## Your choices

You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings, use a content blocker, or install the [Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on](https://tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout). The site works the same way without analytics cookies.

## Links to other sites

Posts link to other websites such as Sitecore's documentation, GitHub and social networks. Those sites have their own privacy practices, which this page doesn't cover.
